## Definition
A subset $H$ of a [[Group (Definition)|group]] $G$ is a **subgroup**, written $H \le G$, if $H$ is itself a group under the operation inherited from $G$. Every group has the **trivial subgroup** $\{e\}$ and the **improper subgroup** $G$ itself; any other subgroup is called **proper**.

## Properties
### One-step subgroup test
**Claim.** A nonempty subset $H \subseteq G$ is a subgroup iff for all $a,b\in H$, $a\cdot b^{-1} \in H$.

**Proof.** ($\Rightarrow$) If $H\le G$, closure and inverses inside $H$ give $b^{-1}\in H$ and then $a\cdot b^{-1}\in H$.

($\Leftarrow$) Suppose the condition holds. Since $H\ne\emptyset$, pick $a\in H$; then $a\cdot a^{-1}=e\in H$ (identity). For any $h \in H$, taking $a=e,\, b=h$ gives $e\cdot h^{-1} = h^{-1}\in H$ (inverses). For closure, given $a,b\in H$, we already have $b^{-1}\in H$, so applying the hypothesis to $a$ and $b^{-1}$ gives $a\cdot(b^{-1})^{-1} = a\cdot b \in H$. Associativity is inherited from $G$. So all four group axioms hold in $H$. $\blacksquare$

### Intersection of subgroups is a subgroup
**Proof** (`Tarea II.pdf`, Problem 3). Let $H,K\le G$ and $A = H\cap K$. Since $e\in H$ and $e\in K$, $e\in A$, so $A\ne\emptyset$. For $a,b\in A$, both $a,b\in H$ and $a,b\in K$; since $H,K$ are subgroups, $ab^{-1}\in H$ and $ab^{-1}\in K$, so $ab^{-1}\in A$. By the one-step subgroup test, $A\le G$. $\blacksquare$

### Subgroups of $(\mathbb{Z},+)$
Every proper subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ under addition has the form $d\mathbb{Z} = \{dk : k\in\mathbb{Z}\}$ for some $d\ge 2$ (subgroups of a [[Cyclic Group|cyclic group]] are cyclic). If $18,30,40 \in H$ for a subgroup $H\le\mathbb{Z}$, then $H = d\mathbb{Z}$ where $d = \gcd(18,30,40) = 2$; since $H$ must contain every integer combination of $18,30,40$ (closure and inverses), and $\gcd(18,30,40)=2$ is the smallest positive such combination by Bézout, $H = 2\mathbb{Z}$.

## Examples
- $U(8) = \{1,3,5,7\}$ has proper subgroups $\{1\}$, $\{1,3\}=\langle3\rangle$, $\{1,5\}=\langle5\rangle$, $\{1,7\}=\langle7\rangle$ — see [[Cyclic Group]].
- In [[RSA Cryptosystem]], the set of $r$th powers modulo $n$ forms a subgroup of $U(n)$; the entire security argument for [[Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange]] relies on the subgroup generated by $g$ inside $U(p)$.
