## Definition
A group $G$ is **cyclic** if there exists $a \in G$, called a **generator**, such that every element of $G$ is a power of $a$:
$$
G = \langle a \rangle := \{a^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}.
$$
If $G$ is finite with $|G|=n$, $a$ generates $G$ iff $|a|=n$ (see [[Order of an Element]]).

## Properties
### $\langle a \rangle = \langle a^{-1} \rangle$
**Proof** (Ejercicios Módulo I, Problem 11). Every element of $\langle a^{-1}\rangle$ has the form $(a^{-1})^k = a^{-k}$ for some $k\in\mathbb{Z}$, which is an integer power of $a$, hence lies in $\langle a \rangle$; so $\langle a^{-1}\rangle \subseteq \langle a\rangle$. Conversely, every element of $\langle a\rangle$ has the form $a^k = (a^{-1})^{-k} \in \langle a^{-1}\rangle$, so $\langle a\rangle \subseteq \langle a^{-1}\rangle$. Hence the two sets are equal. $\blacksquare$

### A group can be non-cyclic while every proper subgroup is cyclic
**Example / proof** ($U(8)$, `Tarea II.pdf`, Problem 4). $U(8)=\{1,3,5,7\}$ has order $4$, so it is cyclic only if some element has order $4$. Direct computation: $|1|=1$, and $3^2=9\equiv1$, $5^2=25\equiv1$, $7^2=49\equiv1 \pmod 8$, so $|3|=|5|=|7|=2$. No element has order $4$, so **$U(8)$ is not cyclic**. Its proper subgroups are $s_1=\{1\}$ (trivial, cyclic), $s_2=\langle3\rangle=\{1,3\}$, $s_3=\langle5\rangle=\{1,5\}$, $s_4=\langle7\rangle=\{1,7\}$ — all of order $2$ and hence cyclic (every group of prime order is cyclic, generated by any non-identity element).

### $U(2^n)$ is not cyclic for $n \ge 3$
**Proof sketch** (Tarea II Gran Final, Problem 5; standard result). Every odd $j$ satisfies $j^2 \equiv 1 \pmod 8$: writing $j=2t+1$, $j^2 = 4t(t+1)+1$, and $t(t+1)$ is always even (consecutive integers), so $8 \mid (j^2-1)$. Consequently every power $j^k$ is congruent mod $8$ only to $1$ (if $k$ even) or to $j$ (if $k$ odd) — no element of $U(2^n)$, $n\ge3$ (a multiple of $8$), can reach all four residue classes $1,3,5,7 \pmod 8$ by taking powers of a single generator. Since $U(2^n)$ always contains elements of all four classes mod $8$ for $n\ge3$, no single element generates the whole group, so $U(2^n)$ is not cyclic. (Structurally, $U(2^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_{2^{n-2}}$ for $n\ge3$, which is non-cyclic since $\gcd(2,2^{n-2})\ne1$.)

## Examples
- $\mathbb{Z}_n$ under addition is cyclic, generated by $1$ (or by any $k$ with $\gcd(k,n)=1$).
- $U(p)$ for prime $p$ is always cyclic (a fact used implicitly whenever [[Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange]] or [[ElGamal Encryption]] picks a generator $g$ of $\mathbb{F}_p^*$).
- The subgroup generated by a base point $G$ on an [[Elliptic Curve over a Finite Field|elliptic curve]] is cyclic of order $n = |G|$, exactly analogous to $\langle g\rangle \le U(p)$ — this is what makes [[ECDH]] and [[ECDSA]] structurally identical to their $U(p)$ counterparts.
