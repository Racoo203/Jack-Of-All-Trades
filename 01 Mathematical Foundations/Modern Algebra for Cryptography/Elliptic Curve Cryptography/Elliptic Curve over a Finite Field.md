## Definition
Fix a prime $p>3$ and $c_1,c_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p := \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$. The **elliptic curve** $E$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ (the course's notation; the more common form writes $y^2=x^3+ax+b$ with $a=c_1,\,b=c_0$) is the set of solutions
$$
E : y^2 = x^3 + c_1 x + c_0 \pmod p
$$
together with a special **point at infinity** $\mathcal{O}$, which acts as the identity for the [[Elliptic Curve Group Law]]. A point $P=(x_1,y_1)$ is **on** $E$ if $y_1^2 \equiv x_1^3+c_1x_1+c_0 \pmod p$.

The curve is required to be **non-singular**, i.e. $4c_1^3 + 27c_0^2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$ (no repeated roots of the cubic), so that the [[Elliptic Curve Group Law|group law]] is well defined at every point (in particular, no point of the curve has $y=0$ and a vertical tangent line issue).

## Properties
### Verifying membership by direct substitution
The only check needed is arithmetic: substitute $(x_1,y_1)$ into $y^2$ and $x^3+c_1x+c_0$ and compare mod $p$.

**Worked example** (`A01722401_Actividad_Criptografía_Elíptica.pdf`): for $E: y^2=x^3+2x+3 \pmod{13}$ and $P=(3,6)$:
$$
6^2 = 36 \equiv 10, \qquad 3^3+2\cdot3+3 = 36 \equiv 10 \pmod{13},
$$
so $P \in E$. The same check confirms $Q=(4,7) \in E$.

### The point group
The set of points of $E$ (including $\mathcal{O}$) forms a finite Abelian group under the [[Elliptic Curve Group Law]]. Its order $|E(\mathbb{F}_p)|$ is close to $p+1$ (Hasse's bound: $\left||E(\mathbb{F}_p)| - (p+1)\right| \le 2\sqrt p$), though this course does not compute it directly — instead, a base point $G$ is given together with its order $n$ (e.g. $G=(4,20)$ of order $n=11$ on $E:y^2=x^3+x+13 \pmod{29}$ in `ECDSA.pdf`).

## Examples
- $E: y^2=x^3+x+1 \pmod{17}$ with base point $G=(9,5)$ — used for [[ECDH]] in `ECDH.pdf`.
- $E: y^2=x^3+x+13 \pmod{29}$, generator $G=(4,20)$ of order $n=11$ — used for [[ECDSA]] in `ECDSA.pdf`.
- The cyclic subgroup $\langle G \rangle$ generated by a base point plays the same role here as $\langle g \rangle \le U(p)$ does in [[Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange]]; recovering the scalar $k$ from $P$ and $kP$ is the [[Elliptic Curve Discrete Logarithm Problem]].
