## Definition
Consider
$$(P)\qquad\min f(x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad g_i(x)\le0\;(i=1,\dots,n),\qquad h_j(x)=0\;(j=1,\dots,m).$$
A constraint $g_i$ is **active** at $x$ if $g_i(x)=0$. A feasible $x^*$ is **regular** if the gradients of the active constraints (and all $h_j$) at $x^*$ are linearly independent (LICQ).

**KKT theorem (necessary conditions).** If $x^*$ is a local minimum of $(P)$ and is regular, there exist multipliers $\lambda_i$ and $\mu_j$ such that
$$\begin{aligned}
&\text{(stationarity)} && \nabla f(x^*)+\sum_i\lambda_i\nabla g_i(x^*)+\sum_j\mu_j\nabla h_j(x^*)=0,\\
&\text{(complementary slackness)} && \lambda_i\,g_i(x^*)=0\quad\forall i,\\
&\text{(dual feasibility)} && \lambda_i\ge0\quad\forall i,\qquad \mu_j\text{ unrestricted},\\
&\text{(primal feasibility)} && g_i(x^*)\le0,\quad h_j(x^*)=0.
\end{aligned}$$
With the Lagrangian $L(x,\lambda,\mu)=f+\sum\lambda_ig_i+\sum\mu_jh_j$, stationarity reads $\nabla_xL=0$ ("Lagrangian technique", Tema 7). For a **maximization** problem, either minimize $-f$ or flip the sign of $\lambda$'s condition.

## Properties
### Where the conditions come from (Tema 7's construction)
One inequality, $\min f$ s.t. $g_1(x)\le0$. Two cases:
- **Inactive** ($g_1(x^*)<0$): locally the constraint does not matter, so $\nabla f(x^*)=0$; set $\lambda_1=0$.
- **Active** ($g_1(x^*)=0$): a direction $d$ is a feasible descent direction if $d^T\nabla f<0$ and $d^T\nabla g_1<0$ (moving inside). No such $d$ exists iff $\nabla f$ and $\nabla g_1$ point in **opposite** directions: $\nabla f(x^*)=-\lambda_1\nabla g_1(x^*)$ with $\lambda_1\ge0$. (Tema 7 writes the second condition as $d^T\nabla g_1\ge0$; the non-strict sign is immaterial for the conclusion, but the *strict inward* direction is $d^T\nabla g_1<0$ for $g_1\le0$.)

Both cases are summarized by $\nabla f+\lambda_1\nabla g_1=0$, $\lambda_1\ge0$, $\lambda_1g_1=0$. With several constraints, $-\nabla f$ must lie in the **cone** generated by the gradients of the active constraints: if one is active, $\nabla f$ is parallel to it; if two are active, $-\nabla f$ lies "between" them; if none, $\nabla f=0$. A rigorous proof uses [[Farkas' Lemma]].

### Sufficiency under convexity
If $f$ is convex (concave, for maximization), every $g_i$ is convex, every $h_j$ is affine, and $x^*$ is feasible and satisfies KKT, then $x^*$ is a **global minimum**.
*Proof.* For feasible $x$, using convexity (first-order inequality, see [[Convex Function]]), $\lambda_i\ge0$ and $g_i(x)\le0$:
$$f(x)\ge f(x)+\sum\lambda_ig_i(x)+\sum\mu_jh_j(x)=L(x,\lambda,\mu)\ge L(x^*,\lambda,\mu)+\nabla_xL(x^*)^T(x-x^*)=L(x^*,\lambda,\mu)=f(x^*),$$
where $L(\cdot,\lambda,\mu)$ is convex as a nonnegative combination, $\nabla_xL(x^*)=0$ by stationarity, and $L(x^*)=f(x^*)$ by complementary slackness and $h(x^*)=0$. ∎

### Solving by hand: enumerate active sets
For each subset of constraints assumed active ($\lambda_i\ne0$ ⇒ $g_i=0$; others $\lambda_i=0$), solve the equations, then discard any candidate with a negative $\lambda_i$ or that is infeasible. With $n$ inequalities there are $2^n$ cases.

## Examples
**Tema 7.** $\min f(x,y)=(x-2)^2+2(y-1)^2$ s.t. $x+4y\le3$, $x\ge y$.
Standard form: $g_1=x+4y-3\le0$, $g_2=y-x\le0$.
$$L=(x-2)^2+2(y-1)^2+\lambda_1(x+4y-3)+\lambda_2(y-x).$$
KKT: $2(x-2)+\lambda_1-\lambda_2=0$; $4(y-1)+4\lambda_1+\lambda_2=0$; $\lambda_1,\lambda_2\ge0$; $\lambda_1(x+4y-3)=0$; $\lambda_2(y-x)=0$; plus feasibility.
Sufficiency: $H_f=\operatorname{diag}(2,4)$ is PD ⇒ $f$ convex; the constraints are linear ⇒ KKT points are global minima.
- *Case 1* ($\lambda_1=\lambda_2=0$): $(x,y)=(2,1)$, but $2+4=6>3$ — infeasible.
- *Case 2* ($\lambda_1=0$, $y=x$): $2(x-2)=\lambda_2$ and $4(x-1)=-\lambda_2$ ⇒ $6x=8$, $x=y=\frac43$, $\lambda_2=-\frac43<0$ — rejected (and $x+4y=\frac{20}3>3$, infeasible anyway).
- *Case 3* ($\lambda_2=0$, $x+4y=3$): $x=2-\frac{\lambda_1}{2}$, $y=1-\lambda_1$ ⇒ $2-\frac{\lambda_1}2+4-4\lambda_1=3\Rightarrow\lambda_1=\frac23$, so $(x,y)=\left(\frac53,\frac13\right)$. Check $x\ge y$ ✓, $\lambda_1>0$ ✓. **KKT point**, $f=\frac19+\frac89=1$.
- *Case 4* (both active): $x=y=\frac35$; then $\lambda_1=\frac{22}{25}$, $\lambda_2=-\frac{48}{25}<0$ — rejected.

**Conclusion: the unique KKT point, hence the global minimum, is $(x^*,y^*)=\left(\frac53,\frac13\right)$ with $f^*=1$.** The Tema 7 slide's final line states the minimum is $x=\frac35$, $y=\frac35$ — that contradicts its own case analysis (Case 4 was rejected; $f(\frac35,\frac35)=1.96+0.32=2.28>1$). Treat the slide's last line as a typo.
