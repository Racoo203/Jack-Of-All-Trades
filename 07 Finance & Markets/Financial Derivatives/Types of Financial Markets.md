## Definition
Financial instruments divide into **underlying assets** (stocks, bonds, commodities, currencies) and their **[[Derivative contract|derivatives]]**. The main markets for underlying assets are:
- **Stock markets** (exchanges), e.g. Wall Street, London Stock Exchange.
- **Bond markets**, dealing in government and corporate debt.
- **Currency (foreign exchange, FX) markets**, where currencies are bought and sold.
- **Commodity markets**, trading physical assets (oil, gold, wheat, etc.).
- **Future and option markets**, on which derivatives are bought and sold.

## Properties
### Shares and IPOs
A private company can raise money by an **initial public offering (IPO)**, selling shares to investors; the company then becomes "owned" by its shareholders, who receive a **dividend** if the company is profitable. After the IPO, shares trade on the stock market and their price reflects expected future dividends and capital growth.

### Bonds
A **bond** is a loan to a government or company; the bondholder receives a **coupon payment** (like interest), usually annually/semi-annually/quarterly, and the **face value** (principal) back at maturity. Example: a 10-year bond, face value \$1{,}000{,}000, 6% annual coupon pays \$60{,}000/year for 10 years, then \$1{,}000{,}000 at maturity. Bonds are considered more cautious than shares (fixed income) but carry **default risk**.

### Money market rate
The instantaneous (overnight) rate paid on a short-notice bank deposit is the **money market rate** (short-term interest rate); money-market accounts are treated as essentially risk-free.

### Commodities market
Trades physical resources (oil, gold, copper, wheat, cattle); complications include storage costs, quality, insurance, and delivery costs.

### Currency (FX) market
An interbank, over-the-counter (OTC) market. The rate for immediate delivery is the **spot rate**. Prices are quoted with a bid/offer spread (e.g. a dollar might be bought at R8.8612 and sold at R9.0862 in South African rand).

### Indices
An **index** is a (usually weighted-average) measure derived from a set of underlying assets — most commonly stocks (e.g. FTSE 100, DJIA), but also bonds and commodities.
