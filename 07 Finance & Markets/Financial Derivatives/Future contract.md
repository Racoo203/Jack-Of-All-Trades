## Definition
A **future contract** is, like a [[Forward contract]], an agreement between two parties to buy or sell an asset at a certain time in the future for a certain price. Unlike forwards, futures are **exchange-traded**: the exchange standardises the contract (amount of asset, quotation convention, daily price-move limits, delivery quality/location) and typically the counterparties never know each other's identity.

## Properties
### Margin requirements
Futures require both sides to post an **initial margin** (a deposit, as a sign of good faith). Daily gains/losses are settled at the close of each trading day as the **variation margin** — a process called *marking to market*. For example, if the futures price falls by \$50 in a day, the holder pays \$50 and the issuer receives \$50; this continues daily throughout the contract's life. Forward contracts, by contrast, carry no margin requirement and are normally traded OTC.
